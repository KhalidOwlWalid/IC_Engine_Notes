From Fundamentals of Internal Combustion Engine, pg. 33

It was mentioned that the average piston speed, $U_p$ will be in the range of:
- $U_p$  = 5 to 15 m/s

with large diesel engines on the low-end (5 m/s) and high performance automobile engines on the high end (15 m/s).

Calculations of obtaining $U_p$ can be obtained from equation taken from [[Theory/Fundamentals of Combustion Engine/Chapters/2. Operating Characteristics/Equations#^A3]]. From  [[Theory/Fundamentals of Combustion Engine/Chapters/2. Operating Characteristics/Equations#^A2]] and [[Theory/Fundamentals of Combustion Engine/Chapters/2. Operating Characteristics/Equations#^A3]]. We can re-arranged the equation to obtain:

$$
U_p = 4aN
$$
where:
- $U_p$ is the average piston speed
- a is the crank offset (distance between the crank pin to the crankshaft center)
- N is the engine speed

Reminder:
$$
R = r/a
$$
where:
- r is the connecting rod length
- a is the crank pin distance from the crankshaft centre spinning axis

$U_p$ ratio is given by:
$$
U_p / \bar{U_p}
$$
where:
- $U_p$ is given by the instantaneous piston speed
- $\bar{U_p}$ is the average piston speed

It is worthy to note that, from the book, it is mentioned that:
- Small engines has an R of 3~4
- Larger engines has an R of 5~10
